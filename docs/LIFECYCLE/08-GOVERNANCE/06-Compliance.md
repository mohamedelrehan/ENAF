# Compliance

## Purpose

The Compliance stage within Governance ensures that the enterprise operates in accordance with applicable laws, regulations, industry standards, contractual obligations, and internal policies.

Compliance is more than satisfying audit requirements. It provides assurance that business processes, technology platforms, security controls, operational practices, and architectural decisions consistently support legal obligations, corporate governance, and organisational objectives.

Within the Enterprise Network Architecture Framework (ENAF), compliance is treated as a continuous governance capability that protects the organisation while enabling innovation and business growth.

---

# Compliance Objectives

The Governance stage aims to:

- Ensure adherence to applicable laws and regulations.
- Enforce enterprise policies and standards.
- Protect organisational assets and information.
- Demonstrate accountability to stakeholders.
- Reduce legal, financial, and operational risk.
- Support internal and external audits.
- Improve organisational trust and transparency.
- Maintain continuous compliance throughout the transformation lifecycle.

---

# Compliance Scope

Compliance applies across every business and technology domain.

| Domain | Compliance Focus |
|---------|------------------|
| Business | Corporate governance, financial controls, contractual obligations |
| Enterprise Architecture | Architecture standards, technology lifecycle governance |
| Network | Connectivity standards, resilience, configuration governance |
| Cloud | Cloud governance, data residency, shared responsibility |
| Security | Information security, identity, access management, cyber resilience |
| Applications | Secure development, licensing, software lifecycle |
| Data | Privacy, retention, classification, data governance |
| Operations | Service management, operational procedures, change governance |
| Vendors | Third-party risk, contractual compliance, supplier assurance |
| Finance | Budget governance, procurement controls, financial reporting |

---

# Internal Compliance

Internal compliance ensures that the organisation follows its own governance framework and operating standards.

Typical areas include:

- Enterprise Architecture Principles
- Technology Standards
- Security Policies
- Operational Procedures
- Change Management Processes
- Risk Management Policies
- Procurement Standards
- Business Continuity Plans
- Disaster Recovery Procedures

Governance regularly reviews adherence to these internal requirements.

---

# External Compliance

External compliance ensures that the organisation meets applicable legal and regulatory obligations.

Examples may include:

- General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
- NIS2 Directive
- ISO/IEC 27001
- ISO 22301
- PCI DSS
- Industry-specific regulations
- National cyber security requirements
- Contractual obligations
- Customer compliance requirements

The specific requirements will vary depending on the organisation's industry and geographical footprint.

---

# Compliance Activities

Governance performs compliance activities throughout the enterprise lifecycle.

Typical activities include:

- Reviewing compliance status.
- Monitoring regulatory changes.
- Conducting compliance assessments.
- Coordinating internal audits.
- Supporting external audits.
- Managing compliance exceptions.
- Tracking corrective actions.
- Reporting compliance performance.
- Updating governance policies.
- Maintaining compliance evidence.

Compliance is monitored continuously rather than only during formal audit periods.

- High-risk non-compliance areas.
- Corrective action progress.
- Security compliance metrics.
- Architecture compliance results.
- Vendor assurance status.

---

# Managing Non-Compliance

Not all compliance issues require immediate remediation, but every issue should be assessed and managed appropriately.

The governance process typically includes:

1. Identify the issue.
2. Assess business impact and risk.
3. Determine root cause.
4. Define corrective actions.
5. Assign ownership.
6. Track remediation.
7. Verify resolution.
8. Capture lessons learned.

Where immediate remediation is not possible, formally approved exceptions should be documented, reviewed, and monitored.

# Success Indicators

Compliance governance is effective when:

- Regulatory obligations are consistently met.
- Internal policies are followed.
- Architecture compliance remains high.
- Audit findings are reduced over time.
- Compliance exceptions are formally managed.
- Corrective actions are completed on schedule.
- Compliance reporting is accurate and timely.
- Executive leadership has confidence in governance controls.

---

# Relationship to the ENAF Lifecycle

Compliance supports every stage of the ENAF lifecycle.

- **Strategy** establishes governance principles and regulatory considerations.
- **Current State** identifies existing compliance gaps.
- **Target State** defines future compliance capabilities.
- **Architecture** embeds compliance into standards and reference architectures.
- **Roadmap** plans compliance initiatives and investment priorities.
- **Implementation** delivers compliant solutions.
- **Operations** maintains compliant day-to-day services.
- **Governance** continuously monitors and enforces compliance.
- **Optimisation** improves compliance maturity and governance effectiveness.

Compliance therefore acts as a continuous assurance capability rather than a single project activity.

---

# Summary

Compliance within ENAF is an integral part of enterprise governance, providing the controls, oversight, and assurance required to operate responsibly and sustainably.

By continuously monitoring adherence to policies, standards, regulations, and contractual obligations, Governance protects the organisation from unnecessary risk while enabling confident decision-making, maintaining stakeholder trust, and supporting long-term business success.
