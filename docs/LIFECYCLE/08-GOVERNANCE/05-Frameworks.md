# Frameworks

## Purpose

The Governance stage draws upon recognised industry frameworks, standards, and best practices to establish effective enterprise oversight, decision-making, accountability, and continuous alignment between business strategy and technology.

ENAF does not replace these frameworks. Instead, it provides a practical governance model that integrates their strengths into a single enterprise transformation lifecycle.

By leveraging established governance practices, organisations can improve consistency, reduce risk, strengthen compliance, and make informed business decisions while maintaining the flexibility to adapt governance to their own organisational structure.

---

# Governance Framework Landscape

Governance within ENAF combines guidance from multiple industry frameworks.

| Framework | Primary Governance Contribution |
|-----------|---------------------------------|
| COBIT | Enterprise governance, control objectives, accountability, performance management |
| TOGAF | Architecture governance, Architecture Board, compliance assessments |
| ITIL 4 | Service governance, continual improvement, service management practices |
| ISO/IEC 38500 | Corporate governance of information technology |
| ISO 27001 | Information security governance and management |
| ISO 31000 | Enterprise risk management principles |
| NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) | Cybersecurity governance and risk oversight |
| PMBOK / PRINCE2 | Portfolio, programme, and project governance |
| FinOps Framework | Cloud financial governance and optimisation |

ENAF brings these governance disciplines together into a cohesive enterprise governance capability.

---

# COBIT

COBIT provides the primary governance model for enterprise information and technology.

Within ENAF it supports:

- Enterprise governance structures.
- Decision-making processes.
- Performance measurement.
- Accountability.
- Control objectives.
- Governance policies.
- Value delivery.
- Resource optimisation.

Typical COBIT practices include:

- Evaluate, Direct and Monitor (EDM).
- Align, Plan and Organise (APO).
- Monitor, Evaluate and Assess (MEA).

COBIT ensures governance focuses on delivering measurable business value rather than simply managing technology.

---

# TOGAF

TOGAF provides the governance mechanisms required to maintain enterprise architecture integrity.

Within ENAF it supports:

- Architecture Governance.
- Architecture Board.
- Architecture Compliance Reviews.
- Architecture Decision Records (ADRs).
- Architecture Principles.
- Technology Standards.
- Architecture Exception Management.

TOGAF ensures that enterprise decisions remain aligned with the approved target architecture.

---

# ITIL 4

ITIL provides governance practices for service management and operational excellence.

Within ENAF it supports:

- Service Governance.
- Continual Improvement.
- Change Enablement.
- Incident Management.
- Problem Management.
- Service Performance Measurement.
- Service Level Management.

ITIL helps governance monitor whether operational services continue to deliver expected business outcomes.

---

# ISO/IEC 38500

ISO/IEC 38500 defines principles for the corporate governance of information technology.

Within ENAF it supports:

- Executive accountability.
- Strategic alignment.
- Responsible technology use.
- Governance principles.
- Investment oversight.
- Performance evaluation.

Its principles help executive leadership govern technology in the same way as any other strategic business asset.

---

# ISO 27001

- Information security governance.

- Risk treatment.

# Governance Boards

ENAF recommends establishing governance forums that align business, architecture, operations, and investment decisions.

Typical governance boards include:

| Governance Board | Primary Responsibility |
|------------------|------------------------|
| Executive Steering Committee | Strategic direction and executive decision-making |
| Enterprise Architecture Board | Architecture governance and standards |
| Technology Review Board | Technical direction and technology lifecycle decisions |
| Change Advisory Board (CAB) | Review of significant operational changes |
| Risk and Compliance Committee | Enterprise risk, audit, and regulatory oversight |
| Portfolio Governance Board | Investment prioritisation and programme oversight |
| Security Governance Committee | Cybersecurity strategy and risk governance |

Each board has clearly defined authority, membership, and decision responsibilities.

# Governance Principles

Regardless of the framework used, effective governance should follow a common set of principles.

- Business-led decision-making.
- Executive accountability.
- Architecture-driven governance.
- Evidence-based decisions.
- Risk-aware planning.
- Transparency and traceability.
- Continuous performance measurement.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Continuous improvement.

These principles provide consistency across the entire enterprise.

---

# Selecting the Right Framework

Not every organisation requires every governance framework.

The appropriate combination depends on factors such as:

- Organisation size.
- Industry sector.
- Regulatory requirements.
- Enterprise maturity.
- Technology landscape.
- Risk profile.
- Strategic objectives.

ENAF allows organisations to adopt the frameworks that best support their governance model while maintaining a consistent lifecycle approach.

---

# Relationship to the ENAF Lifecycle

Governance frameworks support every stage of the ENAF lifecycle.

- **Strategy** establishes business direction.
- **Current State** identifies governance gaps and risks.
- **Target State** defines the future governance model.
- **Architecture** establishes principles and standards.
- **Roadmap** governs investment priorities.
- **Implementation** governs delivery and change.
- **Operations** governs service performance.
- **Governance** provides enterprise oversight and accountability.
- **Optimisation** uses governance outcomes to drive continual improvement.

---

# Summary

Governance is most effective when it combines proven industry practices with the organisation's own business objectives and operating model.

By integrating frameworks such as COBIT, TOGAF, ITIL, ISO standards, NIST, PMBOK, PRINCE2, and FinOps, ENAF provides a practical governance approach that strengthens executive decision-making, protects enterprise architecture, manages risk, ensures compliance, and enables continuous business value throughout the enterprise transformation lifecycle.
