# Deliverables

## Purpose

The Governance stage produces the policies, decisions, reports, and oversight mechanisms required to ensure that the enterprise continues to operate in alignment with its strategic objectives, enterprise architecture, regulatory obligations, and business priorities.

Unlike the Implementation stage, which delivers technical capabilities, or the Operations stage, which delivers business services, Governance delivers the organisational control and assurance required to sustain long-term enterprise success.

These deliverables provide executive leadership with the visibility, accountability, and decision support needed to govern the enterprise effectively.

---

# Governance Deliverables Overview

The Governance stage typically produces the following deliverables:

| Deliverable | Purpose |
|-------------|---------|
| Governance Operating Model | Defines how governance is organised across the enterprise |
| Governance Charter | Establishes governance principles, scope, authority, and responsibilities |
| Governance Calendar | Defines recurring governance meetings and review cycles |
| Governance RACI Matrix | Clarifies ownership, accountability, and decision authority |
| Executive Governance Dashboard | Provides a consolidated view of enterprise performance |
| Architecture Compliance Report | Measures compliance with enterprise architecture standards |
| Enterprise Risk Register | Tracks strategic, operational, technical, and security risks |
| Compliance Report | Demonstrates adherence to policies, regulations, and standards |
| Investment Review Report | Evaluates technology investments and business value |
| Governance Decision Register | Records major governance decisions and approvals |
| Improvement Register | Captures governance recommendations and improvement opportunities |
| Optimisation Recommendations | Identifies priorities for the Optimisation stage |

Together, these deliverables provide a complete governance framework for executive oversight and continual enterprise improvement.

---

# Governance Operating Model

The Governance Operating Model defines how governance functions across the organisation.

Typical contents include:

- Governance structure.
- Governance principles.
- Governance processes.
- Decision-making model.
- Governance forums.
- Roles and responsibilities.
- Escalation paths.
- Review cycles.

This document establishes the governance foundation for the enterprise.

---

# Governance Charter

The Governance Charter formally defines the purpose and authority of governance.

Typical contents include:

- Governance objectives.
- Scope.
- Executive sponsorship.
- Governance principles.
- Decision authority.
- Membership.
- Responsibilities.
- Success measures.

The charter ensures consistent governance across all business and technology domains.

---

# Executive Governance Dashboard

Executive leadership requires a consolidated view of enterprise performance.

Typical dashboard content includes:

- Strategic objectives.
- Business KPIs.
- Service performance.
- Architecture compliance.
- Risk status.
- Security posture.
- Financial performance.
- Portfolio status.
- Compliance indicators.
- Improvement initiatives.

The dashboard enables informed executive decision-making.

---

# Architecture Compliance Report

This report confirms whether enterprise solutions continue to align with approved architecture principles and standards.

Typical contents include:

- Compliance score.
- Standards adherence.
- Technology lifecycle status.
- Architecture exceptions.
- Technical debt indicators.
- Reference architecture alignment.
- Recommended corrective actions.

The report protects long-term architectural integrity.

---

# Enterprise Risk Register

The Enterprise Risk Register provides a consolidated view of risks affecting the organisation.

Typical entries include:

- Strategic risks.
- Operational risks.
- Technology risks.
- Cybersecurity risks.
- Vendor risks.
- Financial risks.
- Regulatory risks.
- Business continuity risks.

Each risk should include:

- Description.
- Business impact.
- Likelihood.
- Risk rating.
- Owner.
- Mitigation plan.
- Review status.

---

# Compliance Report

The Compliance Report demonstrates how the organisation meets internal and external obligations.

Typical contents include:

- Regulatory compliance status.
- Internal policy compliance.
- Audit findings.
- Corrective actions.
- Compliance exceptions.
- Certification status.
- Outstanding actions.

This report supports executive assurance and audit readiness.

---

# Investment Review Report

Governance evaluates whether enterprise investments continue to deliver business value.

Typical contents include:

- Investment summary.
- Budget performance.
- Benefit realisation.
- Business outcomes.
- Portfolio health.
- Return on investment.
- Future investment recommendations.

This report supports strategic investment decisions.

---

# Governance Decision Register

Every significant governance decision should be recorded to ensure transparency and traceability.

Typical information includes:

- Decision reference.
- Decision date.
- Governance forum.
- Decision summary.
- Business justification.
- Approvers.
- Related Architecture Decision Records (ADRs).
- Follow-up actions.

Maintaining a decision register creates an auditable history of enterprise governance.

---

# Continuous Improvement Register

Governance identifies opportunities to improve enterprise performance.

Typical entries include:

- Lessons learned.
- Process improvements.
- Technology improvements.
- Governance improvements.
- Automation opportunities.
- Operational recommendations.
- Innovation initiatives.

Each recommendation should include ownership, priority, target date, and implementation status.

---

# Optimisation Recommendations

The Governance stage concludes by identifying initiatives that will be evaluated during the Optimisation stage.

Typical recommendations include:

- Architecture enhancements.
- Service improvements.
- Technology modernisation.
- Security enhancements.
- Cloud optimisation.
- Process simplification.
- Cost optimisation.
- Automation opportunities.
- Capability maturity improvements.

These recommendations become direct inputs to the next stage of the ENAF lifecycle.

# Quality Criteria

Governance deliverables should be:

- Accurate.
- Current.
- Evidence-based.
- Measurable.
- Actionable.
- Traceable.
- Business-focused.
- Consistently maintained.

High-quality deliverables enable confident executive decision-making and effective governance.

---

# Relationship to the ENAF Lifecycle

Governance deliverables consolidate information produced throughout the enterprise lifecycle.

They reference:

- Strategic objectives from the **Strategy** stage.
- Enterprise baselines from the **Current State** stage.
- Future capabilities from the **Target State** stage.
- Architecture standards from the **Architecture** stage.
- Investment priorities from the **Roadmap** stage.
- Delivered capabilities from the **Implementation** stage.
- Operational performance from the **Operations** stage.

The outputs of Governance become the primary inputs to the **Optimisation** stage, where the organisation identifies opportunities to improve capability, increase efficiency, reduce risk, and prepare for the next cycle of enterprise transformation.

---

# Summary

The deliverables produced during the Governance stage provide the visibility, accountability, and assurance required to manage the enterprise effectively.

Rather than focusing on technology alone, these deliverables enable executive leadership to monitor performance, protect enterprise architecture, govern investment, manage risk, ensure compliance, and drive continual improvement.

Within ENAF, Governance deliverables ensure that enterprise transformation remains sustainable, measurable, and aligned with long-term business strategy.
