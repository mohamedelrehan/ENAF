# Inputs

## Purpose

The Governance stage relies on information, evidence, and outcomes produced throughout the previous stages of the Enterprise Network Architecture Framework (ENAF).

Unlike earlier stages, Governance does not create the enterprise direction or deliver technical solutions. Instead, it evaluates whether the organisation continues to achieve its strategic objectives while operating within approved architectural, operational, financial, security, and regulatory boundaries.

These inputs provide the evidence required for informed decision-making, executive oversight, performance management, and continual enterprise improvement.

---

# Primary Inputs

The Governance stage receives inputs from every preceding ENAF lifecycle stage.

| Lifecycle Stage | Governance Input |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Strategy | Business vision, strategic objectives, business drivers, success measures |
| Current State | Enterprise baseline, capability assessments, identified gaps and risks |
| Target State | Target business and technology capabilities, operating model, transformation vision |
| Architecture | Enterprise principles, standards, reference architectures, Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) |
| Roadmap | Transformation roadmap, investment priorities, programme milestones |
| Implementation | Delivered capabilities, project outcomes, implementation lessons learned |
| Operations | Operational performance, KPIs, incidents, risks, compliance results, improvement opportunities |

Together, these inputs provide a complete view of how the enterprise is performing and whether it remains aligned with its strategic direction.

# Business Inputs

Governance requires continuous visibility into business performance.

Typical business inputs include:

- Business strategy updates.
- Corporate objectives.
- Business capability performance.
- Customer satisfaction.
- Business value realisation.
- Business risk assessments.
- Executive priorities.
- Market and industry changes.
- Organisational changes.

These inputs ensure that governance decisions remain business-led rather than technology-led.

Typical inputs include:

- Architecture principles.
- Enterprise standards.
- Reference architectures.
- Approved design patterns.
- Architecture Decision Records (ADRs).
- Architecture compliance assessments.
- Technology lifecycle information.
- Technical debt assessments.
- Architecture exception requests.

These inputs enable Governance to protect architectural integrity across the enterprise.

# External Inputs

Governance must also consider external factors that influence enterprise strategy and operations.

- Economic conditions.

---

# Input Validation

Before governance activities begin, the organisation should confirm that:

- Strategic objectives remain current.
- Enterprise Architecture documentation is up to date.
- Operational reporting is available.
- Performance metrics are accurate.
- Risk and compliance information is current.
- Financial reporting is complete.
- Improvement opportunities have been captured.
- Executive reporting has been prepared.

Incomplete or inaccurate inputs reduce the effectiveness of governance and may lead to poor strategic decisions.

---

# Relationship to the Governance Stage

The Governance stage transforms these inputs into:

- Executive decisions.
- Governance reports.
- Risk treatment plans.
- Investment priorities.
- Architecture compliance outcomes.
- Performance reviews.
- Improvement programmes.
- Strategic recommendations.

These outputs ensure that enterprise transformation continues to deliver measurable business value while preparing the organisation for the Optimisation stage and the next strategic planning cycle.

---

# Summary

The Governance stage depends on accurate, timely, and comprehensive information collected throughout the enterprise lifecycle.

By bringing together strategic, architectural, operational, financial, security, risk, and compliance inputs, Governance provides the evidence required to make informed decisions, protect enterprise value, and guide the organisation's continuous evolution.

Within ENAF, these inputs form the foundation for effective enterprise oversight, accountability, and long-term business success.
