# Inputs

## Purpose

The Operations stage begins only after the successful completion of the Implementation Decision Gate and the formal acceptance of the delivered business capabilities.

Unlike previous lifecycle stages, which primarily consume architectural artefacts and planning documents, Operations inherits the complete operational responsibility for the transformed enterprise.

The inputs to the Operations stage therefore represent far more than documentation.

They represent the formal transfer of ownership from the transformation programme to the operational organisation.

Everything delivered during implementation—including business capabilities, enterprise services, infrastructure, operational processes, governance records, automation, and supporting documentation—becomes the operational baseline from which the organisation delivers ongoing business value.

Understanding these inputs is essential because they establish the foundation upon which Operational Excellence, Service Management, Governance, Security, and Continuous Improvement are built.

---

# Why Inputs Matter

The quality of operational performance is directly influenced by the quality of the information, assets, and knowledge received during transition.

Many organisations struggle after major transformation programmes because operational teams receive incomplete documentation, insufficient knowledge transfer, inconsistent configurations, or unclear ownership.

This often leads to:

- Service instability.
- Slow incident resolution.
- Configuration drift.
- Operational inefficiency.
- Increased business risk.
- Reduced confidence from business stakeholders.
- Higher operational costs.
- Loss of architectural integrity.

ENAF addresses these challenges by defining a structured Operational Handover that ensures every critical business and technical capability is transferred in a controlled, governed, and measurable manner.

Operations therefore begins with a trusted operational baseline rather than rebuilding knowledge after deployment.

---

# The Implementation to Operations Transition

The transition from Implementation to Operations is one of the most significant milestones within the ENAF lifecycle.

During this transition, responsibility moves from transformation delivery teams to operational service owners.

This change is not simply an administrative handover.

It represents the point at which enterprise transformation becomes an operational capability owned by the business.

Before Operations officially begins, the organisation confirms that:

- Business stakeholders have accepted the delivered capabilities.
- Programme objectives have been achieved.
- Projects have been completed or formally transitioned.
- Enterprise Architecture has approved the implemented solution.
- Operational teams have completed readiness activities.
- Governance approvals have been obtained.
- Support processes are established.
- Knowledge transfer has been completed.

Only after these activities have been successfully completed does operational ownership formally transfer.

# Business Inputs

Operations exists to support business capabilities rather than technology alone.

The first responsibility inherited from Implementation is the ownership of the business capabilities delivered through the transformation programme.

Business inputs typically include:

## Approved Business Capabilities

Operations receives responsibility for the business services implemented during transformation.

Examples include:

- Retail services
- Manufacturing services
- Supply chain connectivity
- Enterprise collaboration platforms
- Customer digital services
- Cloud business platforms
- Corporate network services
- Remote access capabilities

These services now become operational responsibilities rather than implementation deliverables.

---

## Business Service Catalogue

Operations receives an approved catalogue describing every production service delivered during implementation.

The catalogue typically defines:

- Service name
- Business owner
- Service owner
- Criticality
- Availability requirements
- Service dependencies
- Support model
- Service level objectives
- Recovery objectives

The service catalogue becomes the foundation of ongoing service management.

## Business Ownership

- Approving operational changes.

Clearly defined ownership ensures that operational decisions remain aligned with business priorities.

# Traceability

One of ENAF's fundamental principles is complete enterprise traceability.

Operations therefore maintains the relationships established throughout every previous lifecycle stage.

Every operational capability should remain traceable back to its original business purpose.

Typical traceability includes:

- Business Strategy → Business Objectives
- Business Objectives → Transformation Initiatives
- Transformation Initiatives → Programmes
- Programmes → Projects
- Projects → Enterprise Architecture
- Enterprise Architecture → Implementation
- Implementation → Operational Services
- Operational Services → Business Outcomes
- Operational Metrics → Governance
- Governance → Continuous Optimisation

Maintaining these relationships enables executive leadership to understand how operational performance directly supports strategic business objectives.

---

# Summary

The Operations stage inherits the complete operational responsibility for the enterprise following successful implementation.

Its inputs extend far beyond technical documentation and infrastructure.

Operations receives the business capabilities, enterprise architecture, governance framework, operational processes, security controls, automation assets, monitoring capabilities, knowledge, compliance evidence, and performance measures required to sustain long-term business value.

Collectively, these inputs form the **Operational Handover Package**, providing Operations with a trusted baseline from which enterprise services can be managed, supported, governed, measured, and continually improved.

By defining these inputs in a structured and comprehensive manner, ENAF ensures that operational teams begin with complete organisational knowledge rather than rebuilding information after implementation. This creates a controlled transition from transformation delivery to operational excellence, protecting the organisation's investment while enabling continuous business improvement throughout the operational lifecycle.
