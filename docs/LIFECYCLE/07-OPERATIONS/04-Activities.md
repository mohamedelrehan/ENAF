# Activities

## Purpose

The Operations stage is responsible for sustaining the business capabilities delivered through enterprise transformation.

Its activities ensure that enterprise services remain secure, reliable, resilient, compliant, and continuously aligned with business objectives throughout their operational lifecycle.

Unlike the Implementation stage, where the primary focus is delivering change, Operations focuses on managing the enterprise environment after ownership has transitioned from the transformation programme.

Operations is therefore a continuous business capability responsible for protecting organisational investment, enabling operational excellence, and providing the operational insight required for future governance and optimisation.

---

# Operating Model

Operations within ENAF follows an integrated operating model.

Rather than treating infrastructure, applications, security, cloud, networks, and service management as isolated disciplines, Operations coordinates these capabilities through one enterprise operating model.

The objective is to ensure that every operational activity contributes to business outcomes while maintaining architectural integrity and governance.

The operating model is based on five continuous responsibilities:

- Operate business services.
- Protect enterprise architecture.
- Maintain operational stability.
- Support controlled business change.
- Continuously improve operational performance.

These responsibilities continue throughout the lifecycle of every enterprise capability.

# Summary

Operations is the longest-running and most enduring stage of the Enterprise Network Architecture Framework.

Unlike projects and transformation programmes, which have defined start and end dates, Operations continues throughout the lifetime of the enterprise.

Its responsibilities extend far beyond maintaining infrastructure or resolving incidents.

Operations protects enterprise architecture, delivers business services, governs operational change, manages security, measures business performance, develops organisational knowledge, drives continuous improvement, and provides the operational intelligence that shapes future enterprise strategy.

Within ENAF, Operations is therefore recognised as a strategic enterprise capability rather than a technical support function.

By integrating operational excellence with governance, performance management, and continuous improvement, ENAF ensures that transformation continues to deliver measurable business value long after implementation has been completed.

The outputs generated by Operations become the primary inputs to the Governance and Optimisation stages, completing the continuous enterprise transformation lifecycle.
