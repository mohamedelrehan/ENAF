# Operations

## Purpose

The Operations stage ensures that the enterprise continues to deliver business value after implementation has been completed.

While the previous lifecycle stages focus on defining strategy, designing architecture, planning change, and delivering new capabilities, Operations is responsible for sustaining those capabilities throughout their operational life.

This stage establishes the people, processes, governance, technology, and operational practices required to provide reliable, secure, and efficient business services on a continual basis.

Operations is not simply about keeping systems running.

It is responsible for protecting the investment made during transformation, maintaining architectural integrity, supporting business growth, managing operational risk, and continuously improving enterprise capabilities.

Within the Enterprise Network Architecture Framework (ENAF), Operations represents the transition from project delivery to long-term business ownership.

---

# Objectives

The Operations stage aims to:

- Transition successfully from implementation into business-as-usual operations.
- Establish clear ownership for enterprise services and operational capabilities.
- Deliver stable, secure, and resilient business services.
- Protect enterprise architecture through operational governance.
- Implement standard operational processes across the organisation.
- Maintain agreed service levels and business continuity.
- Monitor enterprise performance using meaningful business and operational metrics.
- Enable proactive operational management through monitoring and automation.
- Reduce operational risk through governance, compliance, and security.
- Capture lessons learned and drive continuous improvement.

---

# Position Within the ENAF Lifecycle

Operations follows the successful completion of the Implementation stage.

Its purpose is to ensure that newly delivered capabilities become sustainable enterprise services that continue to support business objectives throughout their lifecycle.

The outputs produced during Operations provide essential inputs for Governance and Optimisation while also informing future strategic planning.

```text
Strategy
    ↓
Current State
    ↓
Target State
    ↓
Architecture
    ↓
Roadmap
    ↓
Implementation
    ↓
Operations
    ↓
Governance
    ↓
Optimisation
    ↓
Next Strategy Cycle
```

Operations therefore acts as the bridge between transformation delivery and long-term enterprise management.

---

# Success Criteria

The Operations stage is considered successful when:

- Operational ownership has been formally established.
- Business services consistently meet agreed service levels.
- Enterprise monitoring provides end-to-end visibility.
- Security operations protect enterprise services effectively.
- Governance and compliance processes operate continuously.
- Operational risks are actively managed.
- Automation improves operational efficiency.
- Business stakeholders receive regular operational reporting.
- Continuous Improvement is embedded within day-to-day operations.

Success is measured not only by operational stability but also by the organisation's ability to adapt, improve, and support future business change.

---

# Relationship to Other Lifecycle Stages

Operations does not exist in isolation.

It consumes outputs from previous lifecycle stages and generates valuable information for future enterprise decisions.

| Previous Stage | Contribution to Operations |
|----------------|----------------------------|
| Strategy | Business priorities and objectives |
| Current State | Operational baseline and constraints |
| Target State | Desired operational capabilities |
| Architecture | Standards, principles, and designs |
| Roadmap | Planned transition activities |
| Implementation | Delivered enterprise capabilities |

The outputs from Operations directly support:

| Next Stage | Operational Contribution |
|------------|--------------------------|
| Governance | Performance, compliance, and operational oversight |
| Optimisation | Improvement opportunities, lessons learned, and operational intelligence |
| Strategy | Business insights for the next transformation cycle |

This continuous exchange of information enables ENAF to function as a living enterprise lifecycle rather than a one-time project methodology.

# Summary

Operations is where enterprise transformation delivers long-term value.

It ensures that the capabilities delivered during implementation become reliable, secure, governed, and continuously improving business services.

Rather than marking the end of the transformation journey, Operations establishes the operational discipline, governance, and feedback mechanisms that enable the enterprise to evolve continuously.

Within ENAF, Operations transforms completed projects into sustainable business capabilities and creates the operational intelligence that informs governance, optimisation, and the next strategic planning cycle.
