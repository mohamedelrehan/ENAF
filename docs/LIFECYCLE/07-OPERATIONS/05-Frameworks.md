# Frameworks

## Purpose

The Operations stage does not operate in isolation.

Successful enterprise operations rely on established industry standards, operational best practices, governance frameworks, security models, and enterprise architecture guidance.

ENAF does not introduce a new operational methodology to replace these frameworks.

Instead, it provides an enterprise lifecycle that integrates them into a consistent operational model aligned with business strategy, enterprise architecture, governance, and continuous improvement.

This document identifies the primary frameworks that support the Operations stage and explains how they contribute to operational excellence within ENAF.

---

# ENAF Operational Framework Model

Operations within ENAF combines guidance from multiple recognised frameworks.

Each framework contributes specialised practices while ENAF provides the overarching enterprise transformation lifecycle.

| Framework | Primary Contribution |
|-----------|----------------------|
| ITIL | IT Service Management and Service Operations |
| COBIT | Governance, control, and performance management |
| ISO/IEC 20000 | IT Service Management standard |
| ISO 27001 | Information Security Management |
| ISO 22301 | Business Continuity Management |
| NIST Cybersecurity Framework | Cybersecurity operations and risk management |
| TOGAF | Enterprise Architecture governance and operational alignment |
| Microsoft Cloud Adoption Framework | Azure operational guidance |
| AWS Well-Architected Framework | Cloud operational excellence |
| Google Cloud Architecture Framework | Cloud operations and reliability |
| Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) | Reliability engineering and service resilience |
| DevOps | Collaboration and operational automation |
| FinOps | Cloud financial operations and optimisation |
| IT4IT | End-to-end IT operating model |

Rather than selecting a single framework, organisations should adopt the practices most appropriate to their operational maturity, business objectives, and technology landscape.

---

# ITIL

ITIL provides internationally recognised guidance for managing IT services throughout their operational lifecycle.

Within ENAF, ITIL supports the operational management of enterprise services after implementation has been completed.

Typical ITIL practices used during Operations include:

- Incident Management.
- Problem Management.
- Change Enablement.
- Service Request Management.
- Knowledge Management.
- Service Level Management.
- Availability Management.
- Capacity and Performance Management.
- Monitoring and Event Management.

ENAF uses ITIL to strengthen operational service management while ensuring that service activities remain aligned with enterprise architecture and business objectives.

---

# COBIT

COBIT provides the governance and management framework required to ensure that enterprise operations continue to deliver value while controlling operational risk.

Within ENAF, COBIT supports:

- Governance.
- Operational controls.
- Performance measurement.
- Risk management.
- Compliance monitoring.
- Process maturity assessment.
- Audit readiness.

COBIT complements ITIL by ensuring that operational activities remain aligned with organisational governance objectives.

---

# ISO/IEC 20000

ISO/IEC 20000 defines the international standard for IT Service Management.

Within ENAF it supports:

- Service quality.
- Operational consistency.
- Service lifecycle management.
- Continuous service improvement.
- Service governance.

Organisations pursuing formal IT Service Management certification may use ISO/IEC 20000 as the operational management standard supporting ENAF.

---

# ISO 27001

ISO 27001 provides the Information Security Management System (ISMS) that supports secure enterprise operations.

Within ENAF it contributes to:

- Operational security.
- Risk treatment.
- Security governance.
- Security controls.
- Asset protection.
- Access management.
- Compliance.

Security Operations should continuously maintain alignment with the organisation's approved ISMS.

---

# ISO 22301

ISO 22301 supports Business Continuity Management throughout the operational lifecycle.

Within ENAF it provides guidance for:

- Business continuity planning.
- Disaster recovery readiness.
- Operational resilience.
- Crisis management.
- Recovery validation.
- Business continuity testing.

Business resilience remains an ongoing operational responsibility rather than a one-time project deliverable.

---

# NIST Cybersecurity Framework

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework provides practical guidance for managing cybersecurity across operational environments.

Its core functions support Operations by helping organisations:

- Identify operational risks.
- Protect enterprise assets.
- Detect security events.
- Respond to incidents.
- Recover business services.

NIST complements ISO 27001 by providing an operational perspective on cybersecurity activities.

# TOGAF

- Architecture compliance.

Operations should preserve architectural integrity while enabling controlled business change.

# Site Reliability Engineering (SRE)

Site Reliability Engineering applies software engineering principles to operational reliability.

Within ENAF, SRE supports:

- Reliability engineering.
- Error budgets.
- Service Level Indicators (SLIs).
- Service Level Objectives (SLOs).
- Automation.
- Resilience.
- Operational scalability.

SRE practices are particularly valuable for organisations operating cloud-native and digital platforms.

# IT4IT

IT4IT provides an enterprise operating model for managing IT services from strategy through operations.

Within ENAF it supports:

- Value stream management.
- Service lifecycle integration.
- Operational workflows.
- Tool integration.
- Service automation.

IT4IT complements ENAF by improving operational integration across enterprise management platforms.

---

# How ENAF Uses These Frameworks

ENAF does not require organisations to adopt every framework.

Instead, it provides an enterprise lifecycle that allows organisations to integrate the frameworks most appropriate to their business, industry, regulatory environment, and operational maturity.

The objective is not framework compliance for its own sake, but the consistent delivery of secure, resilient, governed, and business-aligned enterprise services.

---

# Summary

Operations is supported by a broad ecosystem of internationally recognised frameworks and standards.

Each contributes specialist guidance in areas such as service management, governance, cybersecurity, business continuity, cloud operations, automation, and financial management.

ENAF integrates these complementary practices into a single enterprise transformation lifecycle, ensuring that operational activities remain aligned with business strategy, enterprise architecture, governance, and continuous improvement.

By positioning existing frameworks within a unified lifecycle rather than treating them as isolated methodologies, ENAF enables organisations to build a mature, scalable, and business-focused operational capability.
