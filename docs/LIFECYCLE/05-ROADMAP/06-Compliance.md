# Compliance

The Roadmap stage must ensure that the enterprise transformation plan complies with the organisation's governance framework, strategic objectives, regulatory obligations, financial constraints, operational requirements, and enterprise architecture.

Unlike the Architecture stage, which governs **how the enterprise should be designed**, the Roadmap stage governs **how the transformation will be delivered**.

Compliance ensures that transformation initiatives are prioritised appropriately, investment decisions are justified, delivery risks are managed, and the roadmap remains aligned with approved enterprise architecture throughout the transformation journey.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of compliance within the Roadmap stage is to ensure that the enterprise transformation roadmap:

- Aligns with approved business strategy.
- Supports the approved Enterprise Architecture.
- Complies with governance and investment policies.
- Reflects regulatory obligations.
- Balances risk, cost, and business value.
- Supports organisational readiness.
- Enables controlled programme delivery.

---

# Compliance Objectives

During the Roadmap stage, the organisation should ensure that the roadmap:

- Supports the approved Strategy.
- Delivers the approved Target State.
- Follows the approved Enterprise Architecture.
- Aligns with portfolio governance.
- Meets regulatory obligations.
- Reflects organisational priorities.
- Incorporates risk management.
- Supports measurable business outcomes.

---

# Strategic Compliance

Every roadmap initiative should directly support the organisation's approved strategic direction.

Typical considerations include:

- Strategic objectives
- Business priorities
- Executive sponsorship
- Business outcomes
- Success measures
- Strategic principles
- Enterprise vision

Transformation activities that cannot demonstrate strategic value should be challenged before inclusion in the roadmap.

---

# Architecture Compliance

The roadmap must remain aligned with the approved Enterprise Architecture.

Typical considerations include:

- Architecture principles
- Technology standards
- Reference architectures
- Approved technology platforms
- Security architecture
- Network architecture
- Data architecture

Roadmap planning should not introduce initiatives that conflict with approved architectural decisions.

---

# Portfolio and Investment Compliance

Transformation planning should comply with the organisation's portfolio management and investment governance processes.

Typical considerations include:

- Investment approval
- Funding governance
- Budget availability
- Portfolio prioritisation
- Resource allocation
- Business case approval
- Benefits realisation

Investment decisions should be based on measurable business value and strategic importance.

---

# Security Compliance

Security considerations should be reflected throughout the transformation roadmap.

Typical considerations include:

- Zero Trust implementation
- Identity and Access Management
- Security modernisation
- Network security
- Cyber resilience
- Security governance
- Security risk reduction

Security initiatives should be planned as integral components of the transformation rather than separate workstreams.

---

# Regulatory Compliance

The roadmap should support the organisation's regulatory and legal obligations.

Typical considerations include:

- GDPR
- NIS2
- DORA
- PCI DSS
- ISO/IEC 27001
- Industry-specific regulations

Where regulatory deadlines exist, roadmap priorities should reflect those obligations.

---

# Operational Compliance

Transformation planning should support sustainable operational delivery.

Typical considerations include:

- Business continuity
- Service transition
- Operational readiness
- Support model evolution
- Monitoring and observability
- Disaster recovery
- Service management

Operational teams should be prepared to support new capabilities before they enter production.

---

# Organisational Compliance

The roadmap should reflect the organisation's ability to absorb change.

Typical considerations include:

- Business readiness
- Change management
- User adoption
- Training requirements
- Communication planning
- Resource availability
- Organisational capacity

Transformation should be delivered at a pace that the organisation can successfully adopt.

---

# Risk and Compliance

Risk management should be embedded within roadmap planning.

Typical considerations include:

- Strategic risk
- Programme risk
- Delivery risk
- Financial risk
- Technology risk
- Security risk
- Vendor risk
- Regulatory risk

Risks should influence initiative prioritisation, implementation sequencing, and governance decisions.

---

# Compliance Validation

Before completing the Roadmap stage, confirm that:

- Transformation initiatives support the approved Strategy.
- The roadmap aligns with the approved Enterprise Architecture.
- Implementation waves have been prioritised appropriately.
- Investment assumptions have been validated.
- Resource availability has been confirmed.
- Security requirements have been incorporated.
- Regulatory obligations have been considered.
- Organisational readiness has been assessed.
- Transformation risks have been documented and reviewed.
- Governance requirements have been satisfied.

If significant compliance issues remain unresolved, they should be addressed before requesting governance approval through **RM-DG-01**.

---

# Summary

Compliance within the Roadmap stage ensures that the enterprise transformation roadmap is strategically aligned, architecturally governed, financially realistic, operationally achievable, and compliant with organisational and regulatory requirements.

By integrating compliance into roadmap development, organisations establish a trusted delivery plan that balances business value, investment, risk, and organisational readiness while maintaining alignment with the approved Enterprise Architecture.
