# Frameworks

The Roadmap stage applies recognised planning, portfolio management, and governance frameworks to convert the approved Enterprise Architecture into a structured and executable transformation plan.

Unlike previous stages, which focus on defining strategy and architecture, the Roadmap stage focuses on **planning delivery**. It determines how transformation initiatives will be prioritised, sequenced, governed, funded, and measured throughout the transformation journey.

The frameworks used during this stage help ensure that the roadmap is realistic, aligned with business priorities, and capable of delivering measurable business outcomes.

---

# Enterprise Architecture

The approved Enterprise Architecture provides the foundation for roadmap development.

During the Roadmap stage, architecture is used to:

- Identify transformation initiatives.
- Understand implementation dependencies.
- Define implementation priorities.
- Support sequencing decisions.
- Maintain architectural consistency.
- Prevent unnecessary technology divergence.

The roadmap should never introduce initiatives that conflict with the approved enterprise architecture.

---

# Portfolio Management

Portfolio Management provides the governance required to prioritise and balance transformation investments across the organisation.

Typical considerations include:

- Strategic alignment
- Investment prioritisation
- Programme selection
- Portfolio balancing
- Capacity planning
- Benefits management
- Portfolio governance

Portfolio Management ensures that investment decisions maximise business value while remaining within organisational constraints.

---

# Programme and Project Management

The roadmap should align with the organisation's programme and project management methodology.

Typical considerations include:

- Programme governance
- Project sequencing
- Delivery planning
- Stage gates
- Resource planning
- Schedule management
- Risk management

The roadmap provides direction, while individual programmes and projects manage detailed delivery activities.

---

# Change Management

Successful transformation requires effective organisational change management.

Typical considerations include:

- Business readiness
- Stakeholder engagement
- Communication planning
- Training and enablement
- User adoption
- Operational transition
- Change impact assessment

The roadmap should balance technical delivery with the organisation's ability to absorb change.

---

# Risk Management

Risk management supports informed prioritisation and delivery planning.

Typical considerations include:

- Strategic risks
- Programme risks
- Technology risks
- Operational risks
- Security risks
- Third-party risks
- Regulatory risks

Risk should influence implementation sequencing and investment decisions throughout the roadmap.

---

# Benefits Management

Every roadmap initiative should contribute measurable business value.

Typical considerations include:

- Business capability improvement
- Cost optimisation
- Revenue enablement
- Operational efficiency
- Customer experience
- Risk reduction
- Compliance improvement

Benefits should be defined early and tracked throughout implementation.

---

# Financial Planning

Roadmap development should align with organisational investment and budgeting processes.

Typical considerations include:

- Capital expenditure (CapEx)
- Operational expenditure (OpEx)
- Funding availability
- Budget cycles
- Investment approvals
- Cost forecasting
- Return on investment

Financial planning ensures that the roadmap remains achievable within available resources.

---

# Governance

Governance ensures that the roadmap remains aligned with business strategy and enterprise architecture throughout its lifecycle.

Typical considerations include:

- Executive sponsorship
- Transformation governance
- Portfolio governance
- Architecture governance
- Decision Gates
- Exception management
- Performance reporting

Governance provides visibility, accountability, and controlled decision-making across the transformation programme.

---

# Agile Delivery

While the roadmap provides long-term direction, delivery may use Agile practices.

Typical considerations include:

- Incremental delivery
- Product roadmaps
- Release planning
- Continuous feedback
- Value-driven prioritisation
- Iterative improvement

The roadmap defines **what** should be delivered and **when**, while Agile teams determine **how** delivery is executed within each implementation wave.

---

# Performance Measurement

Progress against the roadmap should be monitored using measurable indicators.

Typical considerations include:

- Milestone achievement
- Initiative completion
- Benefits realisation
- Delivery performance
- Budget performance
- Schedule performance
- Risk exposure
- Business outcome achievement

Regular performance reviews allow the roadmap to be adjusted where necessary while maintaining alignment with strategic objectives.

---

# Framework Integration

The Roadmap stage combines multiple disciplines into a single transformation planning approach.

| Framework | Primary Contribution |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Enterprise Architecture | Defines architectural direction and implementation constraints |
| Portfolio Management | Prioritises investment and transformation initiatives |
| Programme & Project Management | Organises delivery into programmes and projects |
| Change Management | Enables business adoption and organisational readiness |
| Risk Management | Identifies and manages transformation risks |
| Benefits Management | Defines and tracks expected business value |
| Financial Planning | Aligns funding with transformation priorities |
| Governance | Provides oversight and decision-making |
| Agile Delivery | Supports iterative implementation within roadmap waves |

Together, these frameworks ensure that the roadmap is strategically aligned, financially viable, architecturally governed, and operationally achievable.

---

# Framework Selection

Organisations may apply different frameworks according to their governance model, industry, and delivery approach.

Examples include:

- TOGAF® for enterprise architecture governance.
- PMI®, PRINCE2®, or MSP® for programme and project management.
- SAFe®, Scrum, or Kanban for Agile delivery.
- ITIL® for operational transition and service management.
- COBIT® for governance and performance management.
- Prosci® or similar approaches for organisational change management.

ENAF does not replace these frameworks. Instead, it integrates them into a single lifecycle that connects business strategy with successful transformation delivery.

---

# Summary

The Roadmap stage combines enterprise architecture, portfolio management, programme governance, financial planning, change management, and delivery practices to create a realistic and executable transformation roadmap.

By integrating these frameworks, organisations can prioritise initiatives, manage dependencies, govern investment, and deliver transformation in a controlled, measurable, and business-focused manner.
