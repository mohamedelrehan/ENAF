# Objectives

The Roadmap stage converts the approved Enterprise Architecture into a structured transformation plan that guides the organisation from its current operating model to the approved Target State.

Rather than defining architectural standards, this stage focuses on **prioritisation, sequencing, governance, and delivery planning**. It ensures that transformation initiatives are delivered in a logical order, aligned with business priorities, available resources, organisational readiness, and expected business value.

The following objectives define the outcomes that should be achieved during the Roadmap stage.

---

# Objective 1 – Prioritise Transformation Initiatives

Identify and prioritise the initiatives required to achieve the approved Target State.

Prioritisation should consider:

- Strategic alignment.
- Business value.
- Risk reduction.
- Regulatory obligations.
- Customer impact.
- Operational improvement.
- Technology modernisation.
- Executive priorities.

The highest-value initiatives should be delivered first whenever practical.

---

# Objective 2 – Define Implementation Waves

Organise transformation initiatives into manageable implementation waves.

Wave planning should:

- Reduce delivery risk.
- Support organisational change.
- Balance workload.
- Deliver measurable value early.
- Enable incremental transformation.
- Maintain business continuity.

Each wave should produce tangible business outcomes before progressing to the next.

---

# Objective 3 – Identify Dependencies

Identify relationships and dependencies between initiatives, programmes, projects, technologies, and business capabilities.

Typical dependencies include:

- Business process dependencies.
- Technology dependencies.
- Data dependencies.
- Security dependencies.
- Infrastructure readiness.
- Vendor deliverables.
- Regulatory milestones.

Understanding dependencies reduces implementation delays and conflicting priorities.

---

# Objective 4 – Establish Delivery Milestones

Define the key milestones that measure progress throughout the transformation journey.

Milestones may include:

- Programme approvals.
- Platform readiness.
- Technology deployments.
- Capability releases.
- Business go-live events.
- Migration completion.
- Operational handover.

Milestones provide clear checkpoints for governance and executive reporting.

---

# Objective 5 – Align Resources and Investment

Ensure that transformation activities are aligned with available people, skills, budgets, and technology resources.

Planning should consider:

- Resource availability.
- Funding cycles.
- Delivery capacity.
- Vendor engagement.
- Procurement lead times.
- Organisational readiness.

This helps create a roadmap that is both ambitious and achievable.

---

# Objective 6 – Manage Transformation Risk

Identify and address risks that may affect successful delivery.

Typical risks include:

- Programme complexity.
- Resource constraints.
- Technology maturity.
- Security considerations.
- Business disruption.
- Third-party dependencies.
- Regulatory obligations.

Risk management should be integrated into roadmap planning rather than treated as a separate activity.

---

# Objective 7 – Support Portfolio Governance

Provide a structured roadmap that enables governance across the enterprise transformation portfolio.

The roadmap should support:

- Executive decision-making.
- Investment planning.
- Programme governance.
- Portfolio management.
- Progress reporting.
- Benefit tracking.
- Change control.

This ensures that transformation remains aligned with organisational objectives throughout delivery.

---

# Objective 8 – Enable Organisational Change

Plan transformation in a way that supports the organisation's ability to adopt change successfully.

Considerations include:

- Business readiness.
- User adoption.
- Training requirements.
- Operational transition.
- Communication activities.
- Change management.

Transformation should be paced to minimise disruption while maximising adoption.

---

# Objective 9 – Measure Business Outcomes

Define how success will be measured throughout the transformation programme.

Typical measures include:

- Business capability improvements.
- Service availability.
- Operational efficiency.
- Customer experience.
- Security improvements.
- Financial benefits.
- Technology simplification.

These measures allow progress to be monitored and benefits to be demonstrated over time.

---

# Objective 10 – Prepare for Implementation

Produce a complete transformation roadmap that provides sufficient direction for the Implementation stage.

The roadmap should clearly define:

- Transformation initiatives.
- Delivery sequence.
- Implementation waves.
- Dependencies.
- Milestones.
- High-level timelines.
- Governance checkpoints.
- Expected business outcomes.

This ensures that implementation teams have a clear, approved plan before delivery begins.

---

# Success Criteria

The Roadmap stage is considered successful when:

- Transformation initiatives have been identified and prioritised.
- Implementation waves have been defined.
- Dependencies have been documented.
- Major milestones have been established.
- Resources and investment have been aligned.
- Risks have been assessed and incorporated into planning.
- Governance and reporting mechanisms have been defined.
- The roadmap provides a realistic and achievable path to the approved Target State.
- Stakeholders agree with the proposed transformation plan.
- The roadmap is ready for approval through **RM-DG-01**.

---

# Summary

The objectives of the Roadmap stage ensure that the approved Enterprise Architecture is translated into a practical, governed, and business-focused transformation plan.

By prioritising initiatives, organising implementation waves, managing dependencies, aligning resources, and defining measurable outcomes, the Roadmap provides the enterprise with a clear and executable path from architectural vision to successful implementation.
