# NorthStar Case Study

# Roadmap

## Purpose

This case study demonstrates how the Roadmap stage of the Enterprise Network Architecture Framework (ENAF) is applied within a real enterprise transformation programme.

Following the successful approval of the Enterprise Architecture through **AR-DG-01**, NorthStar Retail Group now develops a structured transformation roadmap that defines how the organisation will move from its Current State to its approved Target State.

The purpose of the Roadmap stage is to translate architectural direction into a realistic delivery plan by prioritising initiatives, defining implementation waves, identifying dependencies, and aligning resources and investment.

Rather than designing solutions, the Roadmap determines **what will be delivered, when it will be delivered, and in what sequence**.

---

# Business Challenge

NorthStar has completed the strategic planning and architecture phases of its transformation programme.

The organisation now has:

- A clear business strategy.
- A validated Current State.
- An approved Target State.
- A governed Enterprise Architecture.

The remaining challenge is delivering the transformation in a controlled and manageable way.

Attempting to implement every initiative simultaneously would:

- Overstretch delivery teams.
- Increase programme risk.
- Delay business value.
- Create unnecessary operational disruption.
- Exceed available funding and resources.

NorthStar therefore develops a phased enterprise roadmap that delivers transformation incrementally while maintaining business continuity.

---

# Transformation Planning Approach

The Enterprise Architecture and Transformation Office work together to identify every initiative required to implement the approved architecture.

Each initiative is evaluated based on:

- Strategic importance.
- Business value.
- Implementation complexity.
- Technical dependencies.
- Regulatory obligations.
- Resource availability.
- Delivery risk.

This ensures that the roadmap focuses on delivering the greatest business value as early as possible.

---

# Prioritising Transformation Initiatives

Transformation initiatives are grouped into logical programmes and prioritised according to organisational objectives.

Examples include:

- Modernising retail store infrastructure.
- Migrating business applications to cloud platforms.
- Implementing enterprise identity services.
- Standardising network connectivity.
- Introducing Zero Trust security.
- Improving data governance.
- Automating operational processes.

Each initiative is assigned a business owner, a delivery priority, and expected business outcomes.

---

# Analysing Dependencies

Before defining implementation waves, NorthStar identifies dependencies between initiatives.

Examples include:

- Cloud platforms must be available before application migration.
- Enterprise identity services must be established before Zero Trust implementation.
- Network modernisation must support cloud connectivity.
- Data governance capabilities must be available before enterprise analytics.
- Operational monitoring must be implemented before production handover.

Understanding these dependencies allows initiatives to be sequenced logically and reduces delivery risk.

---

# Defining Implementation Waves

The transformation programme is organised into implementation waves.

## Wave 1 – Foundation

The first wave establishes the core capabilities required to support future transformation.

Examples include:

- Cloud platform foundation.
- Core network modernisation.
- Enterprise identity platform.
- Security baseline.
- Initial governance capabilities.

The objective is to create a stable and secure foundation for subsequent delivery.

---

## Wave 2 – Business Modernisation

The second wave delivers major business and technology capabilities.

Examples include:

- Application modernisation.
- Data platform implementation.
- Integration platform deployment.
- Store technology improvements.
- Customer experience enhancements.

Business capabilities begin to realise measurable operational improvements during this phase.

---

## Wave 3 – Optimisation

The final wave focuses on optimisation and enterprise-wide adoption.

Examples include:

- Advanced automation.
- AI-enabled operational insights.
- Enterprise observability.
- Continuous optimisation.
- Business capability refinement.
- Technical debt reduction.

The organisation transitions from transformation to continuous improvement.

---

# Resource and Investment Planning

NorthStar validates that the roadmap is achievable using available resources and funding.

Planning activities include:

- Confirming delivery capacity.
- Aligning budgets with implementation waves.
- Identifying specialist skill requirements.
- Coordinating vendor engagement.
- Planning procurement activities.
- Confirming executive sponsorship.

Investment is released progressively to support each implementation wave rather than funding the entire programme upfront.

---

# Governance and Milestones

Major milestones are defined throughout the roadmap to monitor progress and support executive decision-making.

Examples include:

- Foundation platform ready.
- Identity services operational.
- Cloud migration complete.
- Network standardisation complete.
- Business capability release.
- Operational transition completed.
- Programme benefits realised.

Governance reviews are scheduled at the completion of each implementation wave to confirm readiness before progressing to the next phase.

---

# Stakeholder Validation

The proposed roadmap is reviewed with business and technology stakeholders.

The review confirms that:

- Transformation initiatives support business strategy.
- Priorities are agreed.
- Dependencies have been identified.
- Implementation waves are achievable.
- Resource and funding assumptions are realistic.
- Risks have been assessed.
- Business leaders support the proposed transformation journey.

Minor adjustments are made before governance approval.

---

# Decision Gate

Following completion of roadmap planning, NorthStar submits the transformation roadmap for governance review through **RM-DG-01**.

The governance board confirms that:

- All Roadmap activities have been completed.
- Transformation initiatives have been prioritised.
- Dependencies have been documented.
- Implementation waves have been defined.
- Investment and resource plans are realistic.
- Governance milestones have been established.
- Risks have been assessed and mitigation plans documented.

The enterprise transformation roadmap is formally approved.

The approved Roadmap outputs now become the mandatory inputs for the Implementation stage.

---

# Outcome

By completing the Roadmap stage, NorthStar has transformed its approved Enterprise Architecture into a governed, prioritised, and executable transformation plan.

The organisation now has:

- A prioritised transformation portfolio.
- A complete dependency model.
- Clearly defined implementation waves.
- A realistic enterprise transformation roadmap.
- Resource and investment plans.
- Governance milestones and reporting checkpoints.
- A managed view of transformation risks.

Most importantly, NorthStar now has an approved roadmap that provides every programme and project with a clear delivery sequence, ensuring that transformation is implemented in manageable phases while maintaining alignment with business strategy, enterprise architecture, and organisational priorities.

The programme can now proceed to the Implementation stage, where the approved roadmap will be executed through coordinated programmes, projects, and operational change.
