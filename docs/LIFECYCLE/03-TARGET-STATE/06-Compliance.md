# Compliance

The Target State stage must ensure that the future-state enterprise architecture satisfies all applicable legal, regulatory, security, governance, and organisational compliance requirements.

Compliance is not treated as a separate activity after the architecture has been designed. Instead, compliance requirements should be incorporated into every Target State activity to ensure that the future architecture is secure, resilient, governable, and aligned with organisational obligations from the outset.

The Enterprise Architect is responsible for ensuring that compliance requirements influence architectural decisions throughout the Target State stage.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of compliance within the Target State stage is to ensure that the proposed future architecture:

- Aligns with organisational governance.
- Supports regulatory obligations.
- Protects enterprise information.
- Meets security and privacy requirements.
- Enables audit and operational oversight.
- Reduces future compliance risks.

---

# Compliance Objectives

During the Target State stage the Enterprise Architect should ensure that the future architecture:

- Supports business and regulatory obligations.
- Aligns with enterprise governance policies.
- Incorporates security by design.
- Incorporates privacy by design.
- Supports operational resilience.
- Enables future auditing and reporting.
- Supports organisational risk management.

---

# Governance Compliance

The future architecture should comply with the organisation's governance framework.

Typical considerations include:

- Enterprise Architecture Principles
- Technology Standards
- Design Authority Requirements
- Architecture Review Board Decisions
- Strategic Technology Roadmaps
- Approved Technology Standards

The Target State should remain aligned with approved organisational policies and governance decisions.

---

# Security Compliance

Security requirements should be incorporated throughout the Target State architecture rather than added during implementation.

Typical areas include:

- Identity and Access Management
- Zero Trust Architecture
- Network Segmentation
- Encryption Standards
- Secure Remote Access
- Privileged Access Management
- Security Monitoring
- Incident Response Integration

The Target State should define security capabilities that support the organisation's overall cybersecurity strategy.

---

# Privacy Compliance

Where personal or sensitive information is processed, the Target State should support applicable privacy requirements.

Typical considerations include:

- Data Classification
- Data Ownership
- Data Retention
- Data Minimisation
- Consent Management
- Information Lifecycle Management
- Cross-border Data Processing

Privacy requirements should influence the future architecture from the earliest stages of design.

---

# Technology Compliance

Technology decisions should align with approved enterprise standards.

Typical considerations include:

- Approved Cloud Platforms
- Infrastructure Standards
- Platform Standardisation
- Lifecycle Management
- Vendor Standards
- Automation Standards
- Integration Standards

Technology selections should support long-term sustainability and operational support.

---

# Operational Compliance

The future architecture should support operational governance throughout the service lifecycle.

Typical considerations include:

- Operational Support Model
- Service Management Processes
- Monitoring and Observability
- Capacity Management
- Availability Management
- Backup and Recovery
- Disaster Recovery
- Operational Documentation

Operational requirements should be considered during architecture design to reduce implementation and operational risk.

---

# Regulatory Compliance

The Enterprise Architect should understand which regulations influence the future architecture.

Examples include:

- GDPR
- NIS2
- DORA
- PCI DSS
- ISO/IEC 27001
- Industry-specific regulations

Applicable regulations will vary depending on the organisation, industry, and geographical locations in which it operates.

---

# Risk and Compliance

Compliance should support enterprise risk management.

Typical considerations include:

- Business Risk
- Technology Risk
- Cybersecurity Risk
- Operational Risk
- Third-party Risk
- Regulatory Risk
- Business Continuity Risk

Architectural decisions should reduce identified risks where practical and document any accepted residual risks.

---

# Compliance Validation

Before completing the Target State stage, confirm that:

- Governance requirements have been considered.
- Security requirements have been incorporated.
- Privacy obligations have been addressed.
- Regulatory requirements have been considered.
- Technology standards have been followed.
- Operational requirements have been incorporated.
- Enterprise risks have been reviewed.
- Compliance assumptions have been documented where applicable.

If significant compliance issues remain unresolved, they should be addressed before requesting governance approval.

---

# Summary

Compliance within the Target State stage ensures that the future enterprise architecture is aligned with organisational governance, regulatory obligations, security principles, privacy requirements, technology standards, and operational expectations.

By incorporating compliance into the architecture design process, organisations reduce implementation risk, improve governance, and establish a future-state architecture that is secure, resilient, and ready for approval.
