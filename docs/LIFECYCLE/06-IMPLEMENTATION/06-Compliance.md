# Compliance

## Purpose

The Compliance requirements of the Implementation stage ensure that every transformation programme, project, release, and delivered capability complies with the organisation's approved governance framework, Enterprise Architecture, security policies, regulatory obligations, and quality standards.

Compliance is not a single approval activity performed at the end of delivery.

It is a continuous process that ensures implementation remains aligned with business objectives, enterprise standards, and organisational policies throughout the transformation lifecycle.

The purpose of compliance is to reduce implementation risk, improve delivery quality, and ensure that all solutions are fit for operational use.

---

# Compliance Principles

Implementation compliance is based on the following principles.

## Governance by Design

Compliance activities should be embedded throughout programme and project delivery rather than performed only before production deployment.

## Architecture Compliance

Every solution must comply with the approved Enterprise Architecture, architecture principles, and technology standards.

## Security by Design

Security requirements should be incorporated into every phase of implementation.

## Quality by Design

Quality assurance should be integrated into delivery through continuous validation, testing, and review.

## Business Accountability

Business sponsors remain accountable for ensuring delivered capabilities satisfy business and regulatory requirements.

---

# Compliance Areas

Implementation should address compliance across the following areas.

| Area | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| Strategic Compliance | Maintain alignment with approved business objectives. |
| Architectural Compliance | Ensure solutions conform to the approved Enterprise Architecture. |
| Programme Compliance | Govern programme delivery and reporting. |
| Project Compliance | Ensure projects follow approved governance and delivery standards. |
| Security Compliance | Protect organisational information and services. |
| Operational Compliance | Prepare solutions for operational support. |
| Regulatory Compliance | Satisfy applicable legal and industry obligations. |
| Quality Compliance | Validate that delivered solutions meet defined acceptance criteria. |

---

# Governance Compliance

Implementation activities should comply with approved governance processes.

Typical governance controls include:

- Stage approvals.
- Programme reviews.
- Architecture reviews.
- Design approvals.
- Risk assessments.
- Executive reporting.
- Decision management.
- Exception management.

Governance ensures that implementation remains transparent, controlled, and accountable.

---

# Architecture Compliance

All projects should demonstrate compliance with the approved Enterprise Architecture.

Typical validation includes:

- Architecture principles.
- Technology standards.
- Reference architectures.
- Approved design patterns.
- Solution architecture.
- Integration standards.
- Data architecture.
- Infrastructure standards.

Any deviation should be formally assessed, documented, approved, and recorded through the organisation's governance process.

---

# Security and Risk Compliance

Security should be incorporated throughout implementation rather than treated as a final deployment activity.

Typical security controls include:

- Identity and access management.
- Secure configuration standards.
- Network security.
- Data protection.
- Vulnerability management.
- Security testing.
- Risk assessment.
- Security approval before release.

---

# Quality Compliance

Quality assurance should verify that delivered capabilities satisfy business and technical expectations.

Typical quality activities include:

- Peer reviews.
- Design validation.
- Functional testing.
- Integration testing.
- Performance testing.
- User acceptance testing.
- Operational readiness reviews.
- Release approval.

---

# Regulatory Compliance

Organisations should identify and comply with all applicable regulatory and contractual obligations.

Examples may include:

- GDPR.
- ISO/IEC 27001.
- ISO 22301.
- PCI DSS.
- NIS2.
- Industry-specific regulations.
- Internal corporate policies.

The applicable requirements will vary depending on the organisation, sector, and geographical location.

# Compliance Outputs

Successful compliance activities produce:

- Architecture compliance approval.
- Security approval.
- Risk assessment.
- Quality assurance report.
- Operational readiness assessment.
- Regulatory compliance evidence.
- Governance decisions.
- Approved implementation records.

These outputs provide the evidence required to support implementation governance and release decisions.

---

# Summary

Compliance within the Implementation stage ensures that enterprise transformation is delivered responsibly, securely, and in accordance with approved business, architectural, operational, and regulatory requirements.

Rather than acting as a final checkpoint, compliance is embedded throughout programme and project delivery, enabling organisations to reduce risk, improve quality, and maintain governance while delivering measurable business value.
