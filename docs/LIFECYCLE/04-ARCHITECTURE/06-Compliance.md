# Compliance

The Architecture stage must ensure that the enterprise architecture complies with the organisation's governance framework, regulatory obligations, security policies, technology standards, and operational requirements.

Compliance is an integral part of enterprise architecture and should be incorporated throughout the architecture development process rather than being applied during implementation.

The Enterprise Architect is responsible for ensuring that all architectural principles, standards, reference architectures, and design guidance align with the organisation's compliance obligations before governance approval.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of compliance within the Architecture stage is to ensure that the enterprise architecture:

- Aligns with business and enterprise governance.
- Supports legal and regulatory obligations.
- Applies approved architecture standards.
- Incorporates security and privacy by design.
- Enables consistent solution architecture.
- Reduces implementation and operational risk.
- Supports long-term governance and maintainability.

---

# Compliance Objectives

During the Architecture stage, the Enterprise Architect should ensure that the enterprise architecture:

- Aligns with approved Strategy and Target State outputs.
- Complies with enterprise architecture principles.
- Applies approved technology standards.
- Incorporates security and privacy requirements.
- Supports operational governance.
- Enables auditability and traceability.
- Reduces enterprise architecture risk.
- Supports consistent solution development.

---

# Architecture Governance Compliance

The enterprise architecture should comply with the organisation's architecture governance framework.

Typical considerations include:

- Enterprise Architecture Principles
- Architecture Review Board Requirements
- Design Authority Decisions
- Technology Standards
- Reference Architectures
- Enterprise Design Policies

The Architecture stage establishes the standards that future solution architectures will be expected to follow.

---

# Security Compliance

Security requirements should be embedded throughout the enterprise architecture.

Typical considerations include:

- Zero Trust Architecture
- Identity and Access Management
- Network Segmentation
- Security Monitoring
- Encryption Standards
- Secure Platform Design
- Security Architecture Standards
- Cyber Resilience

The enterprise architecture should provide consistent security guidance across all architecture domains.

---

# Privacy Compliance

Where personal or sensitive information is processed, the enterprise architecture should incorporate applicable privacy requirements.

Typical considerations include:

- Data Classification
- Data Ownership
- Data Protection
- Data Retention
- Data Minimisation
- Information Lifecycle Management
- Cross-border Data Processing

Privacy should be considered as part of the architecture rather than as a project-specific activity.

---

# Technology Compliance

Technology standards should be consistently applied across the enterprise architecture.

Typical considerations include:

- Approved Technology Platforms
- Cloud Standards
- Infrastructure Standards
- Integration Standards
- Automation Standards
- Platform Lifecycle Management
- Vendor Standards

The enterprise architecture should minimise unnecessary technology diversity while supporting business flexibility.

---

# Operational Compliance

The enterprise architecture should support long-term operational management.

Typical considerations include:

- Service Management
- Monitoring and Observability
- Capacity Management
- Availability Management
- Backup and Recovery
- Disaster Recovery
- Operational Support Models
- Operational Documentation

Operational requirements should be reflected within the enterprise architecture to ensure future solutions are supportable.

---

# Regulatory Compliance

The Enterprise Architect should understand the regulatory obligations that influence the enterprise architecture.

Examples include:

- GDPR
- NIS2
- DORA
- PCI DSS
- ISO/IEC 27001
- Industry-specific regulations

The architecture should enable compliance with applicable regulations without unnecessarily increasing complexity.

---

# Risk and Compliance

Compliance activities should support enterprise risk management.

Typical considerations include:

- Strategic Risk
- Technology Risk
- Security Risk
- Operational Risk
- Integration Risk
- Third-party Risk
- Regulatory Risk

Where risks cannot be eliminated, they should be documented and managed through the organisation's governance processes.

---

# Compliance Validation

Before completing the Architecture stage, confirm that:

- Enterprise architecture principles have been applied.
- Architecture standards have been documented.
- Reference architectures are complete.
- Security requirements have been incorporated.
- Privacy obligations have been considered.
- Technology standards have been applied.
- Operational requirements have been addressed.
- Regulatory obligations have been considered.
- Architectural risks have been documented where appropriate.

If significant compliance issues remain unresolved, they should be addressed before requesting governance approval.

---

# Summary

Compliance within the Architecture stage ensures that the enterprise architecture is governed, secure, standardised, and aligned with organisational, regulatory, and operational requirements.

By incorporating compliance into the architecture development process, organisations establish a trusted architectural foundation that supports consistent solution design, reduces implementation risk, and enables effective governance throughout the remainder of the ENAF lifecycle.
