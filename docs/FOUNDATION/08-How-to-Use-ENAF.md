# How to Use ENAF

## Purpose

The Enterprise Network Architecture Framework (ENAF) is designed to be practical, scalable, and adaptable to organisations of different sizes and levels of architectural maturity.

This document explains how organisations, enterprise architects, programme teams, technology leaders, and operational teams can apply ENAF throughout an enterprise transformation initiative.

ENAF is not intended to replace existing organisational processes or recognised industry frameworks. Instead, it provides the enterprise transformation lifecycle that aligns them into a structured, business-driven approach.

---

# Who Should Use ENAF?

ENAF is intended for organisations that are planning, delivering, governing, or operating enterprise network transformation.

Typical users include:

- Enterprise Architects
- Network Architects
- Solution Architects
- Cloud Architects
- Security Architects
- Technology Leaders
- Programme Managers
- Project Managers
- PMO Teams
- Operational Teams
- CIOs and Technology Executives

Although each role has different responsibilities, they all contribute to the same enterprise transformation lifecycle.

---

# When Should ENAF Be Used?

ENAF can be applied whenever an organisation needs to make significant changes to its enterprise network environment.

Typical scenarios include:

- Enterprise network modernisation
- Cloud transformation
- Hybrid and multi-cloud adoption
- SD-WAN and SASE programmes
- Zero Trust implementation
- Data centre transformation
- Mergers and acquisitions
- Enterprise standardisation
- Global network expansion
- Operating model transformation
- Technology refresh programmes

The framework is equally suitable for greenfield and brownfield environments.

# Applying ENAF in Practice

Successful adoption follows a structured sequence.

## Step 1 — Understand the Business

Begin by understanding:

- Business strategy
- Business drivers
- Organisational priorities
- Transformation objectives
- Success measures

Avoid beginning with technology discussions.

---

## Step 2 — Establish the Baseline

Develop a clear understanding of the current environment.

This includes:

- Business capabilities
- Applications
- Infrastructure
- Network
- Security
- Operations
- Risks
- Constraints

The current state provides the foundation for all future decisions.

---

## Step 3 — Define the Target State

Develop a shared vision of the future enterprise.

Define:

- Target capabilities
- Target operating model
- Target architecture
- Enterprise standards
- Success criteria

Every target-state decision should support approved business objectives.

---

## Step 4 — Design the Architecture

Develop the enterprise architecture required to achieve the target state.

Typical activities include:

- Enterprise architecture
- Domain architectures
- Reference architectures
- Standards
- Design patterns
- Architecture decisions

Architecture should remain technology-agnostic and business-driven.

---

## Step 5 — Build the Transformation Roadmap

Plan how the organisation will move from the current state to the target state.

The roadmap should define:

- Transformation initiatives
- Programmes
- Projects
- Transition architectures
- Priorities
- Dependencies
- Milestones

---

## Step 6 — Deliver Transformation

Execute approved programmes and projects.

Implementation should remain aligned with:

- Enterprise architecture
- Governance
- Business priorities
- Standards
- Success measures

Implementation delivers approved architecture—it does not redefine it.

---

## Step 7 — Transition to Operations

Operational teams assume responsibility for delivered capabilities.

Activities include:

- Service transition
- Operational readiness
- Monitoring
- Service management
- Performance management
- Operational reporting

Operations should begin before implementation is complete to ensure a smooth transition.

---

## Step 8 — Govern Continuously

Governance should remain active throughout the lifecycle.

Typical governance activities include:

- Architecture reviews
- Standards compliance
- Risk management
- Decision records
- Exception management
- Programme oversight
- Benefits reviews

Governance provides consistency rather than bureaucracy.

---

## Step 9 — Continuously Improve

Enterprise transformation never truly finishes.

Organisations should continually improve based on:

- Business feedback
- Operational metrics
- Lessons learned
- Governance reviews
- Technology evolution
- Performance analysis

These improvements feed directly into the next strategic planning cycle.

# Applying ENAF at Different Levels

ENAF scales to different organisational needs.

| Scope | Typical Application |
|--------|---------------------|
| Enterprise | Organisation-wide transformation |
| Business Unit | Division or regional transformation |
| Programme | Large transformation programme |
| Project | Strategic project aligned to enterprise architecture |
| Domain | Network, cloud, security, or infrastructure architecture |
| Solution | Individual solution aligned to enterprise standards |

The same lifecycle applies regardless of scope.

ENAF complements existing methodologies.

Examples include:

# Measuring Success

Successful adoption of ENAF should result in:

- Strong alignment between business strategy and technology.
- Consistent enterprise architecture.
- Better governance and decision-making.
- Predictable programme delivery.
- Improved operational readiness.
- Increased security and resilience.
- Reduced technical debt.
- Measurable business outcomes.
- Continuous enterprise improvement.

Success should be evaluated by business value rather than the quantity of documentation produced.

---

# Best Practices

Successful organisations typically:

- Begin with business strategy.
- Establish a trusted architectural baseline.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Govern decisions consistently.
- Standardise wherever practical.
- Deliver transformation incrementally.
- Involve operational teams early.
- Measure business outcomes continuously.
- Treat architecture as an ongoing organisational capability.

---

# Summary

ENAF should be applied as a continuous enterprise transformation lifecycle rather than a sequence of isolated projects.

By following the lifecycle consistently, organisations can align business strategy, enterprise architecture, programme delivery, operations, governance, and continuous optimisation within a single structured methodology.

The result is a business-driven, repeatable, and scalable approach to enterprise network transformation that delivers measurable business value while maintaining architectural integrity, operational excellence, and complete traceability from strategy to outcomes.
